World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi ended with a large prisoner swap agreed but no ceasefire or territory deal; US envoy Jared Kushner said trust was lacking (Reuters). In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's navy said a tanker caught fire after hitting a mine, a day after the Revolutionary Guards said three tankers struck mines — both claims unconfirmed — while Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris that economic pressure made major combat unnecessary, and Iran's security chief said the strait stays shut until the US meets seven conditions (AP/Reuters). At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres told 102 heads of state and government that slow climate action put the world 'on a path to destruction' (UNFCCC/AFP). In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to send the impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, killing it; Mississippi's Senate race stayed uncalled, Democrat Scott Colom ahead by about 2,600 votes, 97% counted (AP). US stocks fell after October inflation ran hotter than expected — consumer prices up 0.4% on the month, no annual rate published — and Nvidia dropped 5.5% as traders priced in a December Fed rate rise (Reuters). A Florida judge let President Trump's $10bn BBC defamation suit proceed, dismissing his unfair-trade claim (BBC). Israel swore in its new Knesset with no government formed; Gadi Eisenkot has until 6 December to build a coalition, Benjamin Netanyahu caretaker meanwhile (Reuters). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; uncharged (Metropolitan Police). Congo's government put Ebola cases at 11,042, with at least 5,387 deaths (Reuters). El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele said his forces destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang's headquarters in Haiti; about 20 reported killed, unconfirmed (BBC). A bus crash in Seongnam, South Korea, seriously injured nine and hurt 48 more in a 32-vehicle pile-up (Reuters).
